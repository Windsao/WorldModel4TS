\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
%======================================================================

\paragraph{Time-series foundation models.}
Moirai \citep{moirai}, Chronos \citep{chronos}, Time-MoE \citep{timemoe}, Timer \citep{timer},
TimesFM \citep{timesfm} and MOMENT \citep{moment} pretrain on large time-series corpora with
tokenisation, quantisation or patching designed for the modality, and are evaluated zero-shot on
held-out datasets. They set both the accuracy we compare against and the resource bar we are trying
to undercut.

\paragraph{Forecasting with visual backbones.}
VisionTS \citep{visionts} folds a series into a periodicity-aligned image and inpaints the
right-hand portion with a frozen ImageNet masked autoencoder \citep{mae}. VisionTS++
\citep{visiontspp} keeps that construction, continues pretraining on LOTSA, and adds colourised
multivariate rendering and nine quantile heads trained with a pinball objective. Two observations
from this line matter for us. The visual prior that survives the transfer appears to be small:
SVTime \citep{svtime} matches VisionTS with a 215k-parameter model and OccamVTS \citep{occamvts}
distils a vision forecaster into $1\%$ of its parameters. And VisionTS++ already supervises numerical
forecasts directly: our objective-mismatch result (Section~\ref{sec:diagnosis}) is a statement about
VideoMAE's native masked-pixel objective continued on rendered series, the configuration we tested,
not a criticism of the image-based systems we compare against.

\paragraph{Video pretraining and future prediction.}
VideoMAE \citep{videomae} reconstructs masked spatiotemporal tubes, so its temporal structure is
implicit rather than a forward model; V-JEPA \citep{vjepa} and V-JEPA~2 \citep{vjepa2} predict
latent representations of masked regions and are used for planning, closer to the world-model
formulation of \citet{lecun2022} and \citet{dreamer}. We use both as initialisations. The sense in which
this paper's training stage is a world model is the operational one stated in the abstract: it maps
an observed past to a predicted future state of the series and is scored on that prediction. That is
the whole of the definition we use, and the title should be read with it. We test no claim about
action conditioning, planning, learned simulation or physical understanding, and we do not describe
the VideoMAE checkpoint itself as any of those things.

\paragraph{What is new here, component by component.}
Each element of Section~\ref{sec:method} is a change relative to a specific existing practice.
\emph{Rendering}: VisionTS folds a series into one image whose rows are periods; we render one
period per \emph{frame}, so the period index becomes the model's temporal axis and the masked region
is a block of future frames rather than a region of a still image. It is the representation all our
arms run in; no experiment isolates it from frame-based prediction. \emph{Supervision}: value-space
losses are standard in forecasting and VisionTS++ trains a quantile objective over values. What is
new is putting a differentiable pixel-to-value read-out \emph{inside} a masked-video pretraining
objective, which converts a video backbone's reconstruction loss into a forecasting loss without
touching the backbone; Table~\ref{tab:ablation} measures it against pixel-only supervision under
matched conditions. \emph{Scale alignment}: multi-scale training is common, but here the training
distribution over periods-per-frame is set equal to the one the inference rule draws from, a
consistency property that is part of the recipe rather than an independently ablated component.

\paragraph{Transfer regularisation.}
L2-SP \citep{l2sp} decays weights toward their initial values rather than toward zero. It is an
existing method used here as an ablation arm: it helps at 16-frame clips and is inert in the
configuration of our main model (Appendix~\ref{app:16f}).
