\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{Visual backbones as forecasters.}
VisionTS \citep{visionts} renders a univariate series into a periodicity-aligned image, masks the
right-hand portion, and lets a frozen ImageNet masked autoencoder \citep{mae} inpaint it; decoding
the completion gives a forecast that is competitive zero-shot with dedicated time-series models.
VisionTS++ \citep{visiontspp} keeps the construction and continues pretraining the backbone on
LOTSA \citep{moirai}, with colourised multivariate rendering and a multi-quantile head; the
continual-pretraining stage is what moves it to the top of the zero-shot table. We share the premise
that a visual backbone is a reasonable prior for forecasting, and we change the objective that the
continual-pretraining stage optimises. To our knowledge this is the first report that the native
masked-reconstruction objective degrades forecasting monotonically as its own loss improves.
Adjacent evidence points the same way: SVTime \citep{svtime} matches VisionTS with a 215k-parameter
model carrying three inductive biases, and OccamVTS \citep{occamvts} distils a vision forecaster
into $1\%$ of its parameters. If most of the transferred capacity can be removed without cost, most
of it was not being used, which is what one expects when the objective that selects the capacity is
not the one that scores the output. RoMAE \citep{romae} is the complementary case: a masked
autoencoder made to work on irregular time series by changing the positional representation it sees
rather than the architecture around it.

\paragraph{Time-series foundation models.}
Moirai \citep{moirai}, Chronos \citep{chronos}, Time-MoE \citep{timemoe}, Timer \citep{timer},
TimesFM \citep{timesfm} and MOMENT \citep{moment} pretrain directly on time-series corpora with
tokenisation, quantisation or patching schemes designed for the modality. They are our baselines,
and the comparison is deliberately unequal in their favour on data: LOTSA in full is 231B
observations, and we use $0.3\%$ of it.

\paragraph{Video models as world models.}
A world model predicts the future state of an environment from its past and an action
\citep{lecun2022,dreamer}. V-JEPA \citep{vjepa} and V-JEPA~2 \citep{vjepa2} instantiate this in
video by predicting the latent representation of masked future regions, and are used downstream for
planning. VideoMAE \citep{videomae} is not a world model in this sense --- it is a bidirectional
masked autoencoder whose prior over dynamics is implicit --- but the distinction turns out to matter
less than the objective it is fine-tuned with. We use both families as backbones and, once the
forecast-aligned objective is applied, both become world models over rendered series in the literal
sense: past frames in, future frames out, supervised on the decoded values.

\paragraph{Transfer and anchoring.}
Continual pretraining of a strong backbone on a narrow corpus risks erasing the prior it was chosen
for. L2-SP \citep{l2sp} regularises toward the initial weights instead of toward zero. We use it as
an ablation arm and report where it matters (16-frame clips) and where it is inert (32-frame clips),
which is itself informative about what the video prior supplies.
