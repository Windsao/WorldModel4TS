\section{A world model over rendered series}
\label{sec:method}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

The model observes the first $F-h_p$ frames of a rendered clip and predicts the last $h_p$. Training
supervises that prediction in the space where forecasts are scored. Everything else in this section
exists to make those two sentences well defined.

\subsection{Rendering: one period per frame}
\label{sec:render}

Let $x_{1:L}$ be a univariate series with dominant period $P$, taken from the sampling frequency as
in \citet{visionts}. We render $L = 16 k P$ observations into $F$ frames of $224\times224$, where
$k$ is the number of periods per frame. Values are standardised with the mean and standard deviation
of the \emph{visible context only}, so no test-time statistic leaks into the input, then clipped to
$z\in[-3,3]$ and mapped to a column height
\begin{equation}
h \;=\; 0.5 + 0.4\,\frac{z}{3} \;\in\; [0.1,\,0.9].
\label{eq:height}
\end{equation}
Column $c$ of a frame is drawn as an area chart: row $\mathrm{round}\big((1-h)\cdot 223\big)$ is the
boundary, rows below it are filled with gray level $0.12$, rows above hold background $0.92$, and
horizontal grid lines every 28 rows carry $0.80$. Frame $f$ holds periods $[fk,(f+1)k)$. Vertical
position therefore encodes value and horizontal position encodes phase within the period, so a
seasonal series becomes a nearly static video and a trending or regime-changing one becomes a moving
one. With $F=32$ and a tubelet size of 2, a clip is 16 temporal by $14\times14$ spatial tokens.

\subsection{Masking: the future, not a random subset}

The last $h_p \in \{2,4\}$ frames are masked; these are the future. With probability $0.3$ the
leading tubelets are hidden as well, so the model also trains on contexts shorter than the full
window, which is the regime it meets at test time on short datasets. To retain some of the original
pretraining signal, $30\%$ of batches instead use standard tube masking. The reconstruction loss is
computed on the masked future tokens only.

\subsection{The forecast-aligned objective}
\label{sec:objective}

Pixel error is a poor surrogate for forecast error because the decoder that turns pixels into
numbers is not in the loop. We put it there. For a predicted frame $\hat{x}$, define the per-pixel
soft occupancy
\begin{equation}
s(\hat{x}_{r,c}) \;=\; \mathrm{clamp}\!\left(\frac{0.80 - \hat{x}_{r,c}}{0.80-0.12},\,0,\,1\right),
\qquad
\hat{h}_c \;=\; 1 - \frac{224 - \sum_{r} s(\hat{x}_{r,c})}{223},
\label{eq:readout}
\end{equation}
which counts, differentiably, how much of column $c$ is filled. The thresholds are the fill level
and the grid-line level from Section~\ref{sec:render}, so grid lines contribute a bounded, constant
offset rather than gradient noise. Inverting Equation~\ref{eq:height} gives
$z = (h-0.5)\cdot 3/0.4$, and the training objective is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} \;=\; \underbrace{\mathrm{MSE}(\hat{x}, x)}_{\text{pixel}}
\;+\;
\lambda \left(\tfrac{3}{0.4}\right)^{2}
\underbrace{\mathrm{MSE}\big(\hat{h}_c,\,h_c\big)}_{\text{value}},
\qquad \lambda = 0.1 ,
\label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
where the constant $(3/0.4)^2$ converts height units into standardised value units, so the second
term is an MSE in exactly the units the benchmark reports. It is computed column-wise and is
therefore independent of $P$, of the dataset, and of the number of periods packed into a frame. The
pixel term is kept because it stabilises early training, when the read-out of a blurry prediction is
uninformative; $\lambda=0.1$ was the first value we tried and $0.3$ is worse
(Appendix~\ref{app:failed}).

This is the whole of the method's gain. It is also what makes the model a world model over rendered
series rather than an inpainter: the quantity being optimised is the future state of the series, in
the units of the series.

\subsection{Frame-scale augmentation and prior anchoring}

At test time a long horizon is covered by packing more periods into each frame, so the model must
work at several values of $k$. During training we sample $k \in \{1,2,4\}$ with probability
$0.5/0.3/0.2$, matching the inference rule below. Independently, we can decay weights toward their
initial values $\theta_0$ rather than toward zero, $p \leftarrow p - \eta\lambda_{\mathrm{sp}}(p -
\theta_0)$ with $\lambda_{\mathrm{sp}}=0.05$ and weight decay off \citep{l2sp}. This matters at
16-frame clips and is inert at 32 (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}), which we read as evidence that one
thing the video prior supplies is long-range temporal context --- something a longer window supplies
directly.

\subsection{Inference}
\label{sec:inference}

One checkpoint and one rule produce every number we report. Given period $P$ and horizon $H$: if
$H \le 4P$, a single pass with $h_p=4$, averaged over the scales $k$ with $kPs \le 96$; otherwise
$k = \lceil H/4P \rceil$ periods per frame, followed by $\lceil k/(96/P)\rceil$ autoregressive
rollout passes when $P<96$ and a single direct pass when $P \ge 96$. There is no per-dataset
tuning, no ensembling over checkpoints and no blending with statistical baselines anywhere in the
paper.

\subsection{Backbone, optimisation and corpus}

The main model continues VideoMAE-B pretrained on Kinetics-400 \citep{videomae} with
\texttt{norm\_pix\_loss} disabled. VideoMAE's position embeddings are sinusoidal, so moving from 16
to 32 frames rebuilds the tables and loads the pretrained weights unchanged. We use AdamW
\citep{adamw} ($\beta = 0.9/0.95$, learning rate $10^{-4}$ with cosine decay and 500 warm-up steps),
batch 32 as micro-batch 8 with 4-step accumulation, and bf16; 60k steps take about 20 hours on one
A40.

The corpus is 98 LOTSA subsets \citep{moirai}: 184{,}013 series and 654.2M observations, with $20\%$
synthetic series mixed in and subsets sampled in proportion to the square root of their size. None
of the six evaluation datasets appears in it. We note one constraint this creates: the corpus
contains Monash \texttt{traffic\_hourly} \citep{monash}, which is the same data as the LSF traffic
benchmark, so traffic is excluded from our evaluation rather than reported as a zero-shot result.
