\begin{abstract}

Zero-shot time-series forecasters are normally bought with data: the strongest open models pretrain
on corpora of tens to hundreds of billions of observations. We show that a video-pretrained backbone
plus a transfer framework designed for forecasting reaches the same accuracy band for a small
fraction of that cost. The obstacle is that the transfer does not happen on its own --- continuing a
video model's masked-pixel objective on rendered series lowers that objective while raising forecast
error --- so we rebuild the adaptation stage around the forecasting task. A series is rendered one
period per frame, which makes motion across frames the evolution from one period to the next; the
model predicts a masked block of future frames from the visible past alone; a differentiable
read-out decodes those frames back into values, and the loss is taken there rather than on pixels;
and training samples exactly the frame scales the inference rule will use. After 60k optimizer
updates on 654M observations --- 18.9 hours on one A40, about $0.3\%$ of the archive and $1/27$ of
the sample presentations behind the strongest visual baseline --- a single checkpoint under a single
rule attains a mean MSE of $0.285$ over six standard long-sequence datasets and four horizons, the
lowest of the models we compare, against $0.289$ for VisionTS++ large; its mean MAE, $0.328$, is
behind that model's $0.326$. Holding everything but the initialisation fixed, the video prior is
worth $3$--$5\%$ at every horizon at a 5k-update budget, and an ImageNet prior catches up by 20k:
what video pretraining buys in this regime is adaptation efficiency, which is precisely the
resource the low-budget setting is short of.

\end{abstract}
