\begin{abstract}

Rendering a time series as an image and forecasting it by inpainting is the recipe behind the
strongest published zero-shot forecasters, all of which inpaint with an ImageNet autoencoder. A
video backbone, pretrained on how signals evolve rather than on how they look, ought to be the
better prior. We find that it is, but only once the transfer objective is changed. Continuing
VideoMAE's masked-pixel pretraining on a corpus of rendered series improves its own held-out loss
monotonically while making forecasts monotonically worse over a $35\times$ range of training budget.
The two never meet, because the read-out that converts pixels back into numbers sits outside the
loss. We put it inside: the backbone is trained as a world model over rendered series, observing
past frames, predicting future ones, and scored by a differentiable read-out that decodes those
frames into standardised values. The budget curve inverts. With period-aligned rendering,
frame-scale augmentation and 32-frame clips, a single checkpoint under a single inference rule
attains $0.285$ mean MSE across the six standard long-sequence benchmarks, ahead of VisionTS++
($0.289$) and VisionTS ($0.315$), using $1/13$ of VisionTS++'s continual-pretraining steps and
$0.3\%$ of its data. Swapping only the initialisation, between image, video and none at all, shows
that what transfers is a prior over dynamics, not capacity and not any particular video objective.

\end{abstract}
