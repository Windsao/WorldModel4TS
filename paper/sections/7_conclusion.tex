\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%======================================================================

A video-pretrained backbone can be turned into a competitive zero-shot forecaster on a small
time-series budget, but not by substitution: its own objective drives forecasting accuracy the wrong
way, and what makes the weights usable is a transfer framework built around the task: period-aligned
frames, a predicted block of future frames, a differentiable read-out that places the loss on values,
and a training scale distribution matched to the inference rule. One checkpoint under one rule then
reaches the lowest mean MSE of the models we compare, after 18.9 GPU-hours on $0.3\%$ of the corpus
behind the strongest visual baseline, while staying behind that baseline on mean MAE. The controlled
study puts the video prior's contribution in adaptation efficiency: worth $3$ to $5\%$ at 5k updates and
matched by an image prior by 20k. At small adaptation budgets the temporal prior pays; the transfer
design is what makes any prior pay at all.
