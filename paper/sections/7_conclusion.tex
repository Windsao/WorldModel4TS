\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

A backbone pretrained on video is a useful starting point for forecasting but not a usable one:
continuing its own objective on rendered series improves that objective while degrading forecasts.
What makes the transfer work is a framework built around the forecasting task --- period-aligned
frames, prediction of a masked future block, a differentiable read-out that puts the loss in value
space, and a training scale distribution matched to the inference rule. With it, one checkpoint and
one rule reach the lowest mean MSE among the models we compare on the six-dataset zero-shot
protocol, after 60k updates on 654M observations. Two results bound that claim: the video
initialisation's lead closes by 20k updates, so what we show is adaptation efficiency rather than a
higher ceiling, and the transfer design is what makes any initialisation usable at all. Next steps: a second benchmark, a longer effective
context for the ETT horizons we lose, and the same read-out on an image backbone.
