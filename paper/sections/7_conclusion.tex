\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

Video backbones do help time-series forecasting, but not through the objective they were trained
with. Continuing masked-pixel pretraining on rendered series improves that objective and degrades
forecasting at the same time, monotonically, over a $35\times$ range of budget. Training the same
backbone as a world model over rendered series --- past frames in, future frames out, supervised
through a differentiable read-out in value space --- inverts the curve and produces the best mean
MSE in the published zero-shot table with a small fraction of the pretraining budget of the models
it displaces. A matched-size comparison with a random-init control locates the transferable
ingredient in a prior over dynamics rather than in capacity or in any one video objective.

The practical reading is that the rendering detour is not the interesting design decision; the loop
between prediction and read-out is. Anything that turns a visual model into a forecaster eventually
converts pixels into numbers, and our results suggest that conversion belongs inside the training
objective rather than after it. The obvious next steps are a second benchmark, a longer effective
context to recover the hourly ETT cells, and porting the value-space loss into the image-model
pipeline it was diagnosed on.
