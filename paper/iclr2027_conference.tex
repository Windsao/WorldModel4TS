\subsubsection*{Reproducibility statement}

Every number in this paper comes from one publicly documented pipeline. The renderer, the
value-space read-out, the trainer and all command-line flags used for each arm are described in
Section~\ref{sec:method} and Appendix~\ref{app:impl}; the evaluation follows the protocol of
\citet{visionts} and was checked against their released checkpoint to three decimals
(Section~\ref{sec:setup}). One checkpoint and one inference rule produce all 24 evaluation cells:
no per-dataset tuning, no checkpoint ensembling, no blending with statistical baselines. The
pretraining corpus is built from public LOTSA subsets \citep{moirai} with a documented subset list,
and none of the six evaluation datasets appears in it. Code, corpus manifest and checkpoints will be
released.

\subsubsection*{Ethics statement}

The work uses public forecasting benchmarks and a public pretraining corpus; no human subjects, no
personally identifying data. Electricity and traffic-like series describe aggregate consumption at
substation level. The main risk we see is the usual one for forecasting models sold as general
purpose: the reported zero-shot accuracy is measured on six datasets in two domains and should not
be read as a guarantee on safety-critical deployments.

\subsubsection*{Use of large language models}

Large language models were used for copy-editing and for scripting routine experiment
orchestration. All experimental design, analysis and claims are the authors'; every number reported
here was produced by the pipeline described above.
